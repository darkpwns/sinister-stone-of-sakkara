# Session S10 — Return to the temple

## Recap
Day 219 midday, Cyfaraun, the Silk Pony Inn. The twelfth descent stripped the temple of the dead's corridor and
southern galleries (1,604gp) but cost Septima, burned by her own flask; a hooded thing's touch withered Quirinus, and
Brennus lies in shock. The party stands one level short of the strength the temple's depth demands (23 of 24).

## Where they went
- Day 219 afternoon: Neris laid Salving Rest on Quirinus, whose strength the hooded thing's touch keeps draining, and
  he and Brennus were carried to the Keepers' hospital to lie in the courtyard under Mityara's ever-burning torch.
  Septima was buried by the Grey Sisters from her own purse; the rest of her coin is held for her kin. Vettius took on
  Decimus, a sharp-witted explorer and mapmaker, met in the market a week before.
- Days 220-221: Brennus rose from his bed whole. Quirinus woke weaker each morning (twice). Lucilla went to the Solar
  Citadel: a vicar of Ammonar, cool at first, warmed to her gift and to her account of the temple below the city whose
  inscription calls the faithful to "Awaken", and let Quirinus lie under the great dome's oculus, where the sun's
  holiness is strongest.
- Day 221, thirteenth descent, while Quirinus lay abed: by the far grate to the warlord's halls, keeping well clear of
  the six-armed statue's dais. In the chapel Vettius and Gnaeus found that the scored grey altar slides in a groove like
  the tomb slabs; it moved at the second heave. Beneath: a gold candelabra, a gold bowl of ancient coins, four gold and
  ivory cups, and a padded cedar box with a great blue gem and two blue metal eggs. When Lucilla lifted the box, the
  two black jackal statues woke wreathed in black flame; Aelia had sung them brave beforehand, and in three rounds
  Vestus' fan, Brennus' axe, Hrodvar's mace and Iulia's mace destroyed both. Four tunnel prawns that came up behind
  fled. Home by half past ten.
- Day 221, in town: Lucilla named one egg a death orb, a fire-bomb of the ancients; it sold for 3,300gp, and its twin
  is kept for the temple. The gold and the gem sold for 5,170gp. Iulia became a priest of the third rank (and her
  prayers to Ammonar, long unspoken, are now counted every morning), Brennus a warrior of the second, Neris a
  daughter of Mityara.
- Day 222 dawn: Quirinus woke no weaker under the oculus. Day 223 dawn: weaker again.
- Day 223: Quirinus, his strength withered to a shadow, left Lucilla's service; he stays under the Citadel's dome. His
  battle axe went to Gnaeus. With the party at full strength without him, Vettius let Decimus go. The party opened an
  account at the Merchant Guildhouse and banked its coin.
- Day 223, fourteenth descent: back to the temple of the dead by the first tomb's shaft. In the embalming chamber south
  of the west balcony (the hooded thing's own room, it seems: it did not return) the spilled urns gave up black pearl
  prayer beads, an ivory pendant, a brooch, an amber amulet and vials of blue natron. Through the crossroads' east door
  to the mosaic gallery of the Awakening and the vestry beyond: record scrolls (Vestus read them: the 480 warriors who
  guard the great hall below the balconies in undeath) and myrrh. South through a cracked gallery into another temple:
  past a gallery of robed figures, the high priests' bedchamber with its mosaic of four sleeping gods, and a
  serpent-handled door. Vettius saw the ward cut in its frame and picked its lock; pulled open on a rope from round the
  corner, it burst in lightning on empty air. Something whirred and swung inside unseen. Two bronze cobras rose from
  beside four great chests and were broken in two rounds. The chests held ancient silver, electrum and gold: 10,200gp.
  A crawling swarm came up behind; the vault door was shut and spiked until it went away. Home by five, every pack
  full.
- Day 223, in town: a tax official asked where the antiquities came from; Lucilla's story (and purse) satisfied him.
  The jewellery, scrolls, myrrh and natron sold for 4,384gp. Vettius became a rogue of the fifth rank, Vestus a
  theurgist of the third, Brennus a swordmaster of the third.

- Day 230: Lucilla spent the day with her old contacts. Asked how treasure hunters keep clear of the tax men, they
  named Liber Faunus, "cheaper than the tax man"; they also spoke of the Silk Pony's mistress, river pirates and a
  lanista's crocodile. The table talked it through and settled on one selling day a month, early in the month, some
  sales on the road, coin kept in the strongbox, and Lucilla's tongue for any official; the fence only as a last
  resort.

- Day 231: Lucilla reckoned a cart of her own on the Türos Tem road would barely pay, and instead spent the day at the
  Guildhouse putting 700gp each into two merchant caravans in her name: one on her textile road, one on the long haul
  to the coast.

- Day 232, fifteenth descent (the first below the strength line for the deepest galleries): down the rope into the
  huge cavern, wide of the mould and the grasshoppers, through the bronze doors to the smooth slab in the deep
  gallery. Iulia called Ammonar's blessing over them; the slab rolled at the fifteenth heave. Inside, a mummy with
  scaled, misshapen skin and, unseen at first, a scaly baboon-thing with a viper's fangs. Half the front rank shrank
  from the mummy's stare; Vestus' spell died in his throat. Lucilla's fire-egg burst in the tomb; the mummy's curse
  broke on Brennus' will; Iulia's prayer drove the baboon-thing cowering, and Brennus' axe and Gnaeus' finished it.
  The mummy fell to Brennus in the fourth round. On the second search a seam in the dais: under the sarcophagus a
  compartment with a silver inkwell, a gilded incense altar and frankincense, a chryselephantine unicorn, a chest of
  1,500 ancient gold, two ginger-scented vials (Aelia named one a healing draught), canopic jars, a long encrypted
  scroll on carved bone handles, and six scroll jars: Vestus read six arcane spells, two of them of the sixth rank.
  Home by ten, the goods kept back for the next month's selling day. Iulia became a curate, Neris a sister-initiate,
  Aelia a versifier. With the party well above the line, Calpurnia and Titus were let go.

- Day 233, sixteenth descent, to the galleries above the deep ones. Giant ants at the cliff kept their distance. The
  family tomb behind a bronze door held a game board of obsidian and quartz and a painted lyre (Lucilla named it a
  lyre of lullabies); Vestus read the grieving father's inscription. Behind its slab the father himself, a mummy in a
  gold circlet, rose ready; Iulia's rebuke held him cowering while four blades cut him down. The last slab, in the
  gallery wall, took fourteen heaves; Vettius heard chanting and a scrape above before it opened, so no one went in.
  A woman's mummy in black leather clung to the ceiling and lashed down with a black whip; she tried to sweep
  Brennus off his feet, cut Vestus and Iulia, and burned to nothing under a rain of military oil. A diamond nose-ring
  in a lacquered box, a scroll on black handles, silver in an amphora, alabaster apes, and the whip itself (+1).
  Home by eleven. Varro, Hrodvar and Marcellus each rose to the second rank; with the party again above its line,
  Phaedra and Gnaeus were let go, and Gnaeus' battle axe passed to Varro. Before the day was out the table
  thought better of it and took them both back: from now on the party keeps its people and hires only when it
  falls below its line.

- Days 233-241, Cyfaraun: a week's rest. Vestus learnt Burning Sparks and read two of the mummies' spellbooks; the
  encrypted one went to the Tower of Knowledge to be named (two weeks). Lucilla's textile caravan paid a little; the
  coast caravan's goods were impounded at customs and will be months coming back.
- Day 241, the first selling day of the new month: everything held back went at once (23,045gp), the tax men none
  the wiser. Lucilla became a mercantilist of the fifth rank, Phaedra a seer of the second.
- Day 241 evening, The Mink: Liber Faunus, who had heard of them, offered eight parts in ten for any elven work and
  his blessing on the Old District grates. The table took it.

- Day 242, seventeenth descent, to the temple of the dead's sealed tombs. Five prawns, maddened by the party's
  shouting, died in the ramp gallery. The ramp slab moved at the first heave: a priest-mummy of Telith (Iulia named
  the earth-goddess) rose sluggishly and was cut down in five rounds; silver urn and plates, pearls, opals and a
  scatter of ancient gold. Behind its north fresco Vettius felt a hidden door and heard running water beyond.
- Then the slab by the crossroads, and the worst fight of the descent: an embalmer-priest, a mummy of Dirgion with a
  golden hook, a crawling mass of grave-worms in his wrappings and a shrieking skull above him. Half the company
  shrank from him; his dread oration chilled the rest; the skull's shriek sent Lucilla and Vettius running. The
  worms brought Marcellus and then Neris down (Hrodvar's hands saved both), Iulia's holy water burned him, and her
  holy circle held the gallery while the others threw every flask of oil they had from beyond it, until his
  strangling prayer broke her hold. Brennus' axe finished the priest and then most of his worms before they brought
  him down too; Gnaeus killed the last, and a healing draught and Hrodvar's hands brought Brennus back. Eight of
  them carried the mummy's rot out of the dark; three were carried. Every one was cured at the temples before dusk,
  for 405gp. Brennus became a hero of the fourth rank, Aelia an archivist of the third. Liber Faunus took the
  temple's relics at eight in ten (2,472gp).

## Who they met
- Liber Faunus, owner of The Mink: lean, ageless, faintly pointed ears, groomed goatee; soft, amused, courteous ("Blood is bad for business, friend. Let us find a price instead."). Friendly.
- Aranth Faunus, his brother, behind The Mink's bar: elven ears, unkempt black hair, eager for tomb-raiders' tales.
- A tax official of the prefecture at the Emporium: sharp-nosed, ink-stained cuffs, "and where, madam, would a merchant find such old work?"; satisfied.
- A banker of the Merchant Guildhouse: plump, bored, then brisk once Lucilla talked figures.
- Rector Drusus of the Solar Citadel (from afar): broad, battle-scarred, guilty to be healing while the legions march; took Quirinus into the Citadel Guard.
- Decimus (explorer, Vettius' henchman): lanky, ink-stained fingers, counts everything twice, quick dry wit.
- A vicar of Ammonar at the Solar Citadel: grey-templed, measured; asked what they had seen before what they wanted.

## Epilogue: Quirinus
For ten mornings Quirinus woke on his pallet under the Solar Citadel's oculus and found himself a little less than he
had been the night before. The acolytes who tended the altars learnt to read it in his face: the mornings he sat up
and asked for bread, the mornings he lay still and counted his breaths. Neris' prayer held him in an unbroken sleep
each night; the light that the iron ring of the dome stores by day and pours back at night lay on him like a blanket.

On the twenty-eighth morning he woke and the cold in his chest had not deepened. On the twenty-ninth, when the dawn
prayers began at the east altar, he stood up for the first time in ten days without a hand on the wall, and stayed
standing to the end of them. The thing's touch had left him a husk of the man who swung the battle axe in the first
tomb, thin-armed, quick to tire, but it had let go.

Rector Drusus, the "Hammer of Ammonar", who was carried here himself from the wars in the west and sent to heal for a
year, had watched him every dusk from the west altar. He did not offer pity. He offered a post: a place among the
Citadel Guard, keeping the doors of the dome that had kept him alive. Quirinus took it.

On day 230 a boy from the Citadel came to the Silk Pony with a message for Lucilla, in Quirinus' blunt hand: he was
on his feet, he was wearing Ammonar's winged sun on a grey tabard, and if the company ever came back out of the dark
with something that needed carrying past the Citadel's doors, they knew where to find him. "Tell Gnaeus to keep the
axe sharp. It did right by me."
