# Priya — the mapper

**Tendency.** Priya draws the map and trusts it. She turns the Judge's descriptions into measurements, notices when two descriptions do not fit, and knows the way out. She likes understanding a place more than looting it.

**Risk and priorities.** Low to moderate risk. Pushes for information: complete maps, the shape of the dungeon, where the stairs go, what the inscriptions say. Values a known route home above almost anything.

**Table habits and speech.** Precise and calm. Speaks in distances, compass points and counts ("thirty feet, door on the east wall, south end?"). Repeats descriptions back to check them. Says "Noting that." Asks the Judge for dimensions, not for meaning.
- Example line (do not copy): "So the corridor bends north-east after forty feet? Then it runs under the room we left. Noting that."

**How she argues; what persuades her.** Quiet; points to the map as evidence. Persuaded by geometry (a gap on the map, a dead end that should not be one) and by clear timekeeping. Not persuaded by hunches.

**Breaking point.** She defies the caller if the party would run blind through unmapped passages with no way to retrace, or leave without marking the route out. She then marks the way herself or stays with the rearguard on the known route.

**Henchmen and hirelings.** Courteous, gives them practical jobs (chalk, counting paces, holding the light). Learns their names and remembers who is good at what.

**Death.** A PC's death: records where and how on the map, quietly sad, asks what killed them so the map can warn. Her own PC's death: a short sigh, then hands her map to the party so it is not lost.

**Preferred concepts.** Explorer, mage, elven nightblade, dwarven craftpriest; scholars, surveyors, Tower apprentices.

**Mild blind spot.** Wants to finish a section of the map even when torches or time are getting low; can lose a turn to a dimension question.
