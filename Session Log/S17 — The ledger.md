# Session S17 — The ledger

## Recap
Day 316, before dawn, Cyfaraun, the Silk Pony. Twelve, all whole. The Brotherhood of Knives is dead and nobody
outside the company knows; its ledger, sealed in the Grey Sisters' vault, names Liber Faunus, the Provost Mentenus and
Selene of the Silk Pony as patrons, with their passwords. The white fish is charmed (a week more at least); the
canoes are hidden at the beach; the black barge's mooring at Tertulla's runs to day 321. Today the company leaves the
Silk Pony for the Solar Citadel.

## Where they went
- Day 316. Over breakfast the table talked over the ledger. Kwame wanted a night as the Brotherhood: "Ruby lips.
  We learn who she wants dead." Tomasz: "Selene has a hundred and fifty people. Not us alone." Ingrid: posing as
  killers for hire is conspiracy, and the Captain loses her office on a crime known in the city; the Temple of
  Justice is the court for this. Rosa's call: the book goes to Prelate Ezelus with Iulia and Neris to vouch; the
  rewards for the dead merchants wait until he has spoken; Liber's name goes to nobody else ("he's our fence").
- The company left the Silk Pony at dawn with everything it owned there: the spare gear from the strongbox and the
  grave goods went to the Grey Sisters' vault; the mule, the donkeys and the cart to the Citadel stables, which took
  them as the Captain's beasts. The Prelate was not sitting; Brother Oppius, a thin curate with ink on his fingers,
  wrote down the Captain's request to be heard "on a matter of murder". The company slept in the Citadel barracks.
- Day 317, in a cold heavy rain. Word from the Temple of Justice: the Prelate will hear the Captain on day 319.
  Gideon: "Two days, and the fish is friendly. The king's tomb." Oil, a sheep for the fish and rations bought.
- Thirty-sixth descent. Down through the sewer caves and the temple halls (a sound of slithering in the dark,
  never seen), the crone stair, the beach; the white fish came up for its sheep. The canoes took them downstream to
  the west-bank grotto and the slab door. Vestus' spell put an ogre's strength in Brennus' arms and the slab rolled
  aside at the first heave. Inside, a black-and-gold antechamber; on a bronze dais four gilded coffins, something
  scraping inside them.
- Iulia raised the rod; the first coffin went still. Then three of the king's guard climbed out, mummified elves in
  bronze scale with curved swords, and half the company froze at the sight of them, Iulia too, at first. They beat at
  the air a hand's breadth from Iulia's shield and could not touch anyone. Four bronze elven warriors came through
  the lion archway from the west; then four more, and a crowned dead king with a sickle-shaped sword, who pointed it
  at Iulia and boomed one word in Ancient Argollëan ("Submit!", Vestus said); nobody else understood it, and nothing
  happened. The rod turned the guards one by one and then the king himself; he cowered while Vestus' thunderbolt,
  Neris' prayer and every magic blade in the company cut at him, and Brennus' axe, struck with Iulia's blessing of
  light, broke him in the eleventh exchange. The turned guards fled into the tomb and came back one by one; each was
  turned again and cut down. Nobody in the company took a scratch. The bronze warriors battered at the ward to the end.
- The king's scale, helm and sword were stripped from him. Vestus read the vault door: "Enter in the name of the
  king"; they said "Marcan" and touched it, and the second heave opened it on a throne, a crown, a gold collar, a
  great fan, gold sandals, a chariot, eight shields and three cedar chests of gold. The gold sarcophagus of the king
  went down to the canoes on four backs. On the crone stair the footprints behind them had vanished. Out by the grate
  before four.
- The payout made Lucilla a venturer of the seventh rank, Vestus a sixth-level mage, and Phaedra, Varro, Hrodvar,
  Marcellus and Gnaeus fifth-level; their wages rose to 400gp at once, the month topped up. Gnaeus, after the
  count, told Lucilla he was done: "Five years under other people's orders. I'll lead my own." He took his share and
  his axe and went. Vestus named the helm and the sword (a short sword +2 that commands); the scale would not yield
  its secret.

- Day 318, thirty-seventh descent, for the rest of the king's goods. The fish took its sheep. In the tomb the eight
  bronze warriors stood where they had been left and came at the company again, and again could not touch it. The
  throne, the chariot in pieces, the eight cedar shields (in the lantern light the grain of the cedar had twisted
  into skulls and strange signs), the canopic shrine and its jars, the vases and the gold picked from the mosaics
  went out on the bearers' backs. In the sewer caves four giant flies came buzzing to the lantern, hung there while
  Brennus roared at them, and drifted off. Out by three. Only the four bronze coffins of the guard remain.

- Day 319. In the morning Doarë's sister opened the vault and the ledger came out of the company's chest. At
  the third hour after noon Prelate Ezelus received the Captain in a bare room behind the courts: grave, a face like
  a marble bust, few words. "State the deed. Then state the proof." Iulia stated it; he read the ledger to the end
  without a flicker, then looked at Iulia and Neris. "You are of the Lawful gods, both of you. Do you answer for the
  rest?" They did. "Then there are people you should meet. Tonight. You will not see the way." The families of the
  dead may be told their dead are dead, he said; who paid, nobody.
- That night the six were bound and blindfolded by clerics of Türas and led a long way down and around. When the
  blindfolds came off they knelt in a frescoed elven hall under a black seven-spoked wheel, before a lean,
  soft-spoken man with grey at his temples and hands that never moved: Destrio. He read the ledger slowly. The
  Brotherhood, he said, was founded by Zene, a Nicean who left his order and was hunted from then on; the order is
  glad she and hers met a just end, and that the beasts who took their faces are dead too. He questioned them one by
  one, then said they were worthy: if they would swear secrecy and obedience to the order's lawful judgments, they
  could be acolytes of the Order of the Eclipse, servants of Calefa's justice. They swore.
- To be more than acolytes, a sacred initiation: a righteous retribution the order itself petitions for the
  forgotten dead in the ledger. He named the three: Liber Faunus, for merchants and a traveller of the Order of
  Travelers; the Provost Mentenus, for two elven travellers; Selene of the Silk Pony, for one of her servants and a
  priestess of Mityara. Rosa: "Not Liber. He's our fence." Ingrid: "Mentenus is already the Captain's business."
  Kwame wanted Selene. Gideon's call: the Provost, when the order says the time is right. Destrio kept the ledger
  for the order's codex. "Nobody knows the Brotherhood is dead. Keep it so. If anyone comes looking to hire it, the
  order wants to know who." Blindfolded again, they were back at the Citadel gate before the third hour.

- Day 320, thirty-eighth descent, late in the morning after the night below the temples. In the temple halls a
  pack of giant rats boiled out of a side passage at the lantern; the front line took them on the blades and ten
  died in the first exchange, the rest fled squealing. One bit Brennus; he shrugged it off. The fish took its sheep.
  Two of the guards' bronze coffins came out of the tomb on eight backs while the bronze warriors hammered at the
  ward. The lantern oil ran out; torches. Coming back, a crawling yellow-brown slime oozed across the passage;
  Brennus, Varro and Aelia hit it with burning oil and Vestus fanned fire over it, and it burned to a crust. Out
  after seven.

- Day 321, in town. Lucilla walked out to Tertulla's landing and paid two more weeks for the black barge under her
  sails (to day 335, past the selling day). Over breakfast Rosa raised the rewards for the dead merchants (240gp).
  Ingrid: "Proof of how they died is the den. The den is the Brotherhood. We swore." Gideon: "We swore. Let it go."
  Rosa let it go, loudly. Oil and torches bought. Vettius spent the day at a wine-shop window across from the
  wizards' Chapterhouse in the Tower District; late in the afternoon the Provost came down to the gate to see off a
  carriage: a man in late middle age, neat, silver-grey, smiling at something the coachman said. Nobody looked
  at the wine-shop.
