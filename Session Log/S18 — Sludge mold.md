# Session S18 — Sludge mold

## Recap
Day 330, evening, Cyfaraun, the Solar Citadel. Twelve, all whole. The new month (day 331) opens the selling day for
the king's tomb goods in the Grey Sisters' vault, and the hiring halls again for a woodsman. Arëtar wants proof from
the Provost's own study; Vestus waits for his interview at the Tower (day 339); the Order waits.

## Where they went
- Night of day 330 at the Citadel: quiet.
- Day 331, the first of the twelfth month, clear and very cold. Revered Mother Doarë was out all day: the vault stayed
  shut and the selling day slipped. Vettius paid the hiring halls to look for a woodsman of any rank up to the
  fourth: one came by afternoon, Cotta, a border-fort outrider with a composite bow, a scimitar, scale and his own
  horse. He reads no trail ("Tracks are the horse's business. I watch the skyline."), but he hears well and rides;
  Vettius took him on at fifty a month, paid to the month's end.
- At breakfast Kwame (Brennus): "Destrio wants to know if anyone comes looking for the Brotherhood. The only door we
  know they used is Vasan's wine stalls." Lucilla spent the day with her old contacts at the Emporium (one tale) and
  stopped at the Nicean stalls. The wine-seller, a stout Nicean widow who calls everyone "dove", knew her as Vasan's
  buyer: "A note came for him yesterday, dove, sealed. He's not been by in weeks. You'll see him before I do." For a
  day's pay it was Lucilla's, with a tale about the Patriarch for nothing.
- The note, unsigned, in a looping silky hand the company had seen before (the letter in the rat-men's warren):
  "Sludge mold. For the house of Vasan Tikala: one commission, the price doubled for haste. The usual place, at
  midnight on the third night from this." Third night: midnight of day 333. Ingrid: "Sludge mold is the Provost's
  word in the ledger." The company sent word to the Order that evening by Brother Oppius at the Temple of Justice:
  who had come looking, and when. No answer by night.
- Day 332, clear and brisk. Doarë was out again. Iulia gave Arëtar the month's report at the Citadel (the warren,
  the letter; nothing new below) and said nothing of the note. Vettius and Cotta walked the wharves by daylight and
  looked at the outlet from above: the old sewer mouth under the Emporium wharves, a slope of rubble and broken
  stone to the water's edge, the wharf alley sixty feet back, and the outlet tunnel itself running up into the dark.
- That evening the table settled the night of day 333. Rosa: "Call: Lucilla goes as Vasan's factor again, hood up,
  eye ready; Brennus and Varro push the barrow with Vasan's own cask, the one we took off him. Vettius and Cotta in
  the rubble. Everyone else sixty feet back in the alley, Iulia's spear dark. We hear the job, we take his money, we
  let him walk. The Order decides the rest." Ingrid: "And if it's the Provost himself, and he sees through us?"
  Gideon: "Then he's a rat-man in a sewer at midnight and the Captain may take him. Neris' circle and Iulia's shield
  keep his kind off. Nobody chases him into the tunnel." Kwame, grinning: "I always wanted to be a murderer."
- Day 333, the selling day at last, very cold and a gale off the lake. Doarë opened the vault in the morning.
  Lucilla turned the king's bronze scale over in the light and named it: chain mail of the old elves, good for a
  little more than steel (+1). Liber's men came to the Grey Sisters' gate with two carts and took the elven goods at
  eight parts in ten, clean coin: the scale (4,000gp), the helm (20,000gp), the gold sarcophagus (16,000gp), both
  crowns (6,400gp each), the four guards' coffins and the gilded coffin (2,800gp each), thrones, chariots, the
  canopic shrine, the basin's pearls, coral and shells, the spices and the rest (97,762gp in all). The gems from the
  gold box and the Brotherhood's chests, its armbands and pendants, the silver rat and the mink furs went on the open
  market at full value (16,310gp); no tax man asked anything. Gnaeus' shares of what was sold after he left are held
  for him at the Guildhouse. Iulia rose to the seventh rank of her order (Prelate). Everyone banked his coin.
- Midnight, day 333, the Nicean outlet, a gale off the lake. Vettius slipped into the rubble unheard but could find
  no good shadow there and lay low; Cotta lay flat behind a broken pier stone. Lucilla stood at the water's edge in
  her hood with Brennus and Varro and the barrow, Vasan's own cask on it; the rest waited sixty feet back in the
  wharf alley. At midnight a tall hooded man came down out of the outlet tunnel without a light and stopped in its
  mouth. He looked once at the rubble where Vettius lay and said nothing of it. Then Lucilla felt something touch the
  inside of her head, the way her own eye touches others, and shut it out. A pause. A cultured, precise voice, bored:
  "Vasan sends a woman now. No matter." The commission: "The new Captain of the Underwatch. The woman Iulia, who
  sleeps at the Solar Citadel. Before the new moon. Six thousand: two now." A purse, heavy, set on the cask. "The
  rest here, at midnight on the third night after she is found." He went back up the tunnel the way he came. Nobody
  followed him. Lucilla walked back to the alley with two thousand in gold and told Iulia she was worth six.
- Day 334, windy. Brother Oppius brought Destrio's word to the Citadel gate: "He has bought knives for one of ours.
  The scale is full. He is yours, as you chose; before he finds other knives. Leave no claim, and let it be known only
  to Calefa." Iulia went to Arëtar with the note and the purse and asked him to let the city think her dead, so the
  Provost would come to pay. The Patriarch heard her out. "An unsigned note, a word in a murderers' book and a hooded
  man's gold. Not enough to set before the Regent against a Provost of the Tower, and I will not tell my brothers a
  lie about my Captain. Bring me what he keeps in his study. And Captain: sleep inside these walls." The table:
  Gideon, "Then it's the Order's way." Ingrid: "Lawfully sanctioned, and the Patriarch did not forbid it." Kwame:
  "The house of Vasan writes back." That afternoon a street boy carried a sealed note to the Tower's storefront, for
  the Provost: "Sludge mold. The house of Vasan to its patron: the bird roosts in the Sun's own house and does not
  leave it after dark. The work can be done, but not at the price. The usual place, midnight, the third night."
- Day 335, warm and grey. Lucilla walked out to Tertulla's and paid two more weeks for the black barge (to day 349).
  Ingrid had silver bought for those whose weapons are plain: a silvered spear for Marcellus, a silvered short sword
  for Aelia (the smiths would not silver arrows).
- Day 336, windy. The table planned the night. Tomasz: "Last time I lay in the open. This time I wait in the
  tunnel, in the black, and when he stops at the mouth I'm behind him." Ingrid: "If anyone feels that touch in his
  head, shut it out. Everyone." Gideon: "Neris and I hold him with the prayer from the alley. Vettius has the king's
  word. Whoever is nearest finishes it." Rosa: "And nobody takes anything off him until he's dead."
- Day 337, drizzle. Nobody new came asking for the Brotherhood. At eleven Vettius slipped into the outlet tunnel,
  scraping a boot on the stones (nobody was there to hear) and folded himself into a black niche ten feet up it. The
  others took the same places as before.
- Midnight. The tall hooded man came down the tunnel past Vettius without seeing him and stopped in the mouth. Before
  he had said a word Varro felt something open his head like a book, tried to shut it and could not. He roared
  "He's in me!" and the night broke. Brennus was across the ten feet in a stride and his axe bit deep into the hooded
  man's side; whatever the man had begun to say died in his mouth. From behind him Vettius spoke the old king's
  word, "Freeze", and he froze, half turned. Lucilla stepped in and cut his throat. It was over before Neris and
  Iulia had finished their prayers in the alley.
- Under the hood, a long clever face with a grey-shot beard: Vettius had seen it at the Chapterhouse gate on day
  321. The Provost Mentenus. A rat bolted from his sleeve up the tunnel and was gone. Under the travelling cloak he
  wore a mage's black cassock with the Tower's collar; on him a belt pouch of gold, a fine black-wired dagger, a
  jade ring carved as a vine, a bronze cap worked with letters, a slim ash wand, a vial of grey liquid, a rune-cut
  black staff, and two keys on a ring tagged "Basement 10" and "4". The company weighted the body with rubble and
  gave it to the river, as the Order asked: no claim, known only to Calefa. Back at the Citadel before the first
  hour of day 338.

- Day 338, clear and brisk. Word went to the Order by Brother Oppius: done. Lucilla turned the Provost's things over
  in the light: the dagger is a good +1 blade, the bronze cap the same kind of helm of tongues they sold to Liber; the
  jade ring, the ash wand and the grey vial told her nothing, nor Vestus and Aelia. The Grey Sisters' door stayed
  shut (Doarë out). The table, in low voices: Ingrid, "Nothing of his leaves our hands in this city this season."
  Rosa, "Twenty thousand in experience sitting in a sack!" Then, quieter: "Fine. Türos Tem, or next year."
- Day 339, very cold. Vestus went to his interview. Not the Provost but the Regent, Aurëus Ambador, square and
  sunburnt and short with everyone, and the Treasurer, Clitus Omnus, with his ledgers; the Provost's chair was empty
  and nobody mentioned it. They read his spellbook, asked about the old elven script and his work on the canoes'
  frames, and admitted him (12). Vestus the Unlikely is a thaumaturge of the Tower of Knowledge: oaths to Istreus, a
  diviner's blue cassock, a key to the Chapterhouse, the libraries of his rank and the basement stair; dues 10gp a
  month. For forty gold the storefront's alchemist named the grey vial: a draught of giant strength. In the lounge
  the thaumaturges talked of nothing but the Provost: not seen since the night before last, the Watch asked to look.
  Caeso Lentulus came to the Citadel gate on his own feet, a patch over the empty eye: "Your names are in my report.
  The Watch owes you, and so do I. Ask for me at the Old District post." He had news: the Tower reported its Provost
  missing yesterday; the Watch has been told to look along the river. He had a lead of his own, a Family man who went
  into the sewers by the dyers' lane; Rosa said, very pleasantly, that the company had business enough. He also had
  a tale about the Imperial Vanguard's Aghilas, suckled by a lioness.
- Day 340, cold, snow flurries. Vettius drew Phaedra's wage from the Guildhouse and paid her to day 369. Brother
  Oppius came at dusk: "Tonight. You will not see the way." Bound and blindfolded, down and around, the six knelt again
  under the black wheel. Destrio did not ask how it was done. "The scale is level. You are deathbringers of the Order
  of the Eclipse, servants of Calefa's justice. The house of Vasan is closed: open it again only at my word." Back at
  the Citadel gate before midnight.
- Day 341, warm and bright above. Forty-second descent, eleven strong (Cotta and Saturnin at the Citadel), for the
  gallery beyond the bronze door the company forced in the autumn. Through the Old District grate, the cesspool and
  the sludge caves (masks on), into the tomb halls, and west through the old fresco hall into the burial gallery.
  Vettius scuffed his way down the steep curving passage south of the cedar door but heard nothing at the bottom:
  a domed tomb, twelve skeletons reclining in niches under a cracked painted sky, each with a bronze sickle-sword and
  shield; in a west alcove a sarcophagus carved as a grim elf, crusted with red garnets. Gideon: "Circle, rod,
  flame. Let them come to us." Neris raised her moving circle at the arch; Marcellus touched a niche with the pole.
- The skeletons came out of the wall and Vestus' fan of fire caught all twelve; Iulia lifted the Rod of the Dawn and
  four fell to dust. The sarcophagus lid slid and the grim elf stood up, wrapped and gilded, and eight of the eleven
  cowered at the sight of it; three kept their heads. Against the circle's edge the dead could do nothing. Brennus and
  Vettius cut down the skeletons that crowded it, Vestus burned the last, Marcellus' flask set the mummy alight and
  Phaedra's flame caught it in the alcove; Iulia's second prayer broke it apart. Pale worms spilled from its belly and
  writhed at the edge of the circle until they crumbled. Nobody was touched.
- Rosa prised the eighteen garnets off the lid; Marcellus took the canopic jars. Ingrid: "Fans spent, the rod spent:
  home." Out by the grate before noon. Ingrid had the garnets kept back: "Twenty-one in a hundred the tax men ask us
  now; five next month."
- Day 342, cold and grey. Doarë opened the vault: the garnets, the jars and the Provost's dagger, ring, cap, wand and
  staff went into the company's chest. Vestus spent the day below the Tower as a thaumaturge, mapping the round
  library and the curved corridor ("Noting that: a grate at the south end"). The new moon came and went.
- Day 343, a gale above. Forty-third descent, the same eleven, for the slab north of the long gallery the company had
  seen beyond the arcosolium. Through the painted gallery with the calm elf's statue and up the ramp into the east
  gallery. Hrodvar rigged the slab and the haulers pulled from the ramp top forty feet off; it gave at the sixth
  heave. Behind it, a wrapped warrior already on its feet, and three more of it: copies that moved through each
  other. Five of the eleven quailed at the sight. Iulia lifted the Rod and spoke, and the warrior and all its
  copies fell apart. In the annex behind it: a floor of silver and gold coins, a lotus bowl, a bronze winged dragon,
  and a vellum roll on sword-hilt handles full of battle diagrams in the old elven script ("Fire," said Vestus, very
  quietly, reading the third column).
- Then the cedar door at the end of the burial gallery: a tomb with a martyr's sarcophagus, amethyst eyes, a heap of
  black scarabs trembling on the dais. Iulia prayed from the doorway inside Neris' circle; the scarabs boiled up,
  flasks and two fans of fire burned them away, and the martyr rose from its coffin. Its prayers of dread shook half
  the company; flask after flask missed. Brennus stepped out to it, Vettius slipped round behind it and drove the
  king's sword in, Vestus' fan caught it, and Marcellus' holy water finished it. No one was touched. The amethysts,
  the jars and the ivory fretwork came home; out by ten. Neris and Varro breathed too much of the sludge on the way
  out and shook it off on the street.
