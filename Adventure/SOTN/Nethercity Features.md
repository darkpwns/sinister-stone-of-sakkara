# Nethercity Features — standing rules

Judge only. The rules that hold everywhere in the Nethercity unless a room says otherwise. Hub: [[SOTN Hub]];
background: [[Nethercity History]]. Pages are SOTN printed pages ("SOTN p. N" = PDF page N + 2). ACKS I rules are
given in their ACKS II form; Judge's calls are marked.

## Ecology (SOTN p. 10)
The sewage from the collapsed cesspit ([[Nethercity A - Entrance Caves]], A1), visitors brought by the teleportation
platform (L4, [[Nethercity L - Abyssal Level Caves]]) and the underground river ([[Nethercity P - Sub-Abyssal River Banks]])
feed a food chain:
- **Decomposers** in the sludge: mustard mould ([[Mustard Mold]], thick in the huge deep cave C2), fungal creepers, sage
  slime, ochre ooze, black blobs, gelatinous masses.
- **Grazers** near the sludge (areas A and C above all): cavern locusts, tunnel prawns, insect swarms of millipedes and
  cave crayfish.
- **Vermin hunters:** giant beetles, giant rats, rat swarms (the bombardier beetle nest in C4 eats the swarms).
- **Troglodytes** (degenerate Thrassian stock bred by the elves) in the deep southern caves (area I) hunt vermin and
  scavenge the sludge.
- **Giant ants** in the abyssal caves (L) farm fungus in empty wall graves on sludge; **white apes** ([[Cave Gorilla]],
  area M) raid the ant farms.
- **Big hunters:** caecilians (C6 eats locusts), carrion horrors, giant flies, pythons, rhagodessae (F19, they prefer
  troglodytes), vipers. Giant crabs, leeches and fish come up the river.
- **Undead** wander or sleep in the tombs and temples; incorporeal spirits drawn by the sinkhole; ghouls from the sewers.
  Areas B, D, G, H, J, K, Q, R, S, T are given over to the undead.
- Before the cesspit broke (329 IY) only a few troglodytes and white apes survived, eating river fish and whatever came
  through the platform; both learned to ride it to hunting grounds outside. Today one exiled ape still remembers it; the
  troglodytes keep a legend of a "magic rock" that carries a brave one to "a cave with no walls or ceiling".

## Construction (SOTN p. 11)
- Everything is cut through **basaltic tuff** (soft, dark red to black). **Ceilings 10’** unless noted: rough in the
  caves, hewn and polished in tombs and temples, some late ones decorated.
- **Floors:** tuff, smoothed; in tombs and temples levelled and tiled (clay or marble, 12” squares). The old earthquake
  left many floors uneven or broken (the room notes say where).
- **Walls:** rough in caves; smooth in tombs and temples, often faced with plaster (frescoes) or marble (mosaics), or
  carved in relief.

## Doors (SOTN p. 11)
Forcing a door is the ACKS II door-forcing throw (Dungeonbashing Expertise improves it); SOTN's penalties apply to it.
| Door | Size | Penalty | Helpers | Found open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stone slab** (rolling cylinder sealing a tomb, 1½-2 tons) | — | **−12** | up to 3 | never |
| **Heavy bronze** (temple entrances; fretwork of horses, snakes, mountains, lotuses, eyes, fists) | 8’ × 4’, or 8’ × 8’ double | **−4** | 1 (2 for double doors) | 5% |
| **Cedar interior** (marble frame, carved handles; important ones inlaid with bronze or gold) | 7’ × 3½’ | none | none | 10% |
- Each helper adds **his unmodified STR bonus** to the forcer's throw. Example (p. 11): a fighter forcing on 6+ with
  three helpers of STR 16, 15, 16 needs 6 + 12 − 2 − 1 − 2 = **13+**.
- A slab-door attempt takes **one full round**; the forcers neither move nor attack. Retry at will.
- **Undead behind a slab wake** when it is disturbed. Opened at the **1st attempt**: the undead are just stirring, still in
  their sarcophagi or resting places. **2nd attempt:** they are out of them. **3rd or later:** ready for battle, cannot be
  surprised; casters may have cast spells to prepare. If the door is then left shut, the undead usually return to
  torpor after **one turn** (but see the mummy reactions below). Leaving and coming back later can regain surprise.
- **Hinges and swing:** bronze and cedar doors open into the next room in alphanumeric order (E1's bronze doors push
  into E2a; from C2a they pull); cedar doors are hinged left.

## Inscriptions (SOTN p. 12)
Written in **Ancient Argollëan**. Knows Ancient Argollëan: reads them, no throw. Knows Classical Argollëan: proficiency
throw **11+**, a natural **1** is a fumble. Knows (common) Argollëan, i.e. Elvish: **16+**, natural **1-3** fumble. The
Judge rolls in secret; a fumble gets a false, misleading or comic translation.

## Sarcophagi (SOTN p. 12)
- Typical: 6’ × 4’ × 3’; **stone** 1½-2 tons, lid 675-750 lb; **bronze** 400-500 lb, lid 75-100 lb.
- Pushing a sarcophagus: as a stone slab door (stone) or a bronze door (bronze).
- **Sliding a lid open:** an action instead of attacking; door-forcing throw at **−4** (stone) or **no penalty**
  (bronze); up to 3 helpers add their unmodified STR bonus.
- A **mummy** slides its own lid as an action instead of moving; it can then attack someone adjacent to the sarcophagus,
  or climb out as an action instead of attacking.

## Sewer sludge (SOTN p. 12)
A river of black sludge runs from the cesspool (A1) down to the abyssal caves (L).
- Exploration movement normal; **charging and running at half rate** in it. **Acrobatics** and **Sneaking** (SOTN's
  Move Silently) throws **−2** in it.
- **Fungal infection:** anyone knocked down or falling prone in it saves **vs Death at +4** (ACKS I Poison) or is
  infected; eating it infects with no save. After **1d8 hours** white pulpy scabs appear: **−2 reaction rolls, −1
  surprise and initiative** (itching). Each following day another save vs Death: failed → a permanent **minor scar**
  (as the Mortal Wounds result); success → no scar this day, still infected. Cured by cure disease magic or a Healing
  proficiency throw to cure disease.
- **Miasma** (rotten eggs): exposed characters save **vs Death every turn** or suffer headaches and short breath:
  **−1 to attack throws and damage** until they rest one turn away from the sludge. A cloth over nose and mouth (or a
  like precaution): save **once an hour at +4**.
- **Combustible in area L only** (the miasma is heavier than air): anyone carrying a torch or open flame over the sludge
  in L has a **1 in 6 chance each turn** of setting off a **6d6 fireball** centred on himself. Casting fireball, wall of
  fire or the like in L sets off a **6d6 fireball covering all the sludge in L**. An explosion burns the miasma off its
  area: flame is safe there until it gathers again in **1d6 days**.
- SOTN's optional variants (p. 12): ignore sludge and miasma for a cinematic game; or "cruel realism" (instant explosion
  on lighting a torch, permanent ability loss from the gas). The campaign uses the middle rules above (Judge's call).

## Sinkholes of evil (SOTN p. 12; ACKS II sinkholes of evil, RR)
- **The whole Nethercity is a shadowed sinkhole of evil.** 10% of the corpses buried here have risen as undead (mostly
  skeletons). A creature killed here has a **10% chance to rise as undead after 1d12 months** unless burned or
  consumed — slain monsters can restock the dungeon. Chaotic casters of reversed divine or necromantic spells cast them
  **as if two levels higher**; necromancy here **+1** to magic research throws; any place serves for **blood sacrifice**.
- **Blighted sinkholes** (marked in the room notes, around chaotic altars): 20% of the corpses there have risen; the
  newly slain rise on a **20% chance after 1d4 days** unless burned or consumed; chaotic reversed/necromantic spells as
  **two levels higher**; **Lawful divine casters turn (ACKS II: rebuke) undead as if four levels lower**; necromancy
  **+2** to magic research throws.

## Teleportation (SOTN p. 14)
The builders warded the place against teleporting tomb-robbers. **Teleport** to, from or through the Nethercity: **+20 to
the d% roll** on the teleportation table. **Dimension door** and other normally errorless teleportation to, from or
within it: roll on the teleportation table as for a place **seen casually**. The wards do not affect the **teleportation
platform (L4)**, the **teleportation disc (N5)** or the **funerary barge of the cults (P11)**. (ACKS II spell names:
check the Spell Index for teleport and dimension door; Judge's call where they differ.)

## Wall graves (SOTN pp. 14-16)
- Burial niches in the rock, plaster-lined and painted, for common worshippers. **Horizontal:** 6’ wide, 3’ deep, 2’
  high, stacked 4 high in a 10’ wall, 6 in a 15’, 8 in a 20’ (12 / 18 / 24 per 20’ of wall). **Vertical:** 6’ tall,
  3’ wide, 2’ deep, not stacked, up to 6 per 20’ of wall.
- **75%** hold the calcified bones of an ancient elf in white linen (not animated unless the room says so). Empty ones:
  crumbled to dust, or risen and walking elsewhere (10-20%). **Empty graves still hold their grave goods.**
- Each bears a name and death date in Ancient Argollëan. Oldest near the entrance (~3200 BE), deepest ~1700 BE; each
  room note gives the era.
- **Grave goods by era:** 3100-2600 BE **1d3 gp** (avg 2) each; 2600-2000 BE **1d8 gp** (avg 4.5); 2000 BE and later
  **4d6 gp** (avg 14). **Looting:** 10 graves per person per turn, yielding 20 / 45 / 140 gp by era. Goods weigh about
  1 st per 100 gp.
- **Detail a grave** (tables in `Tables/`): sex 1d20 (1-10 male, 11-20 female); name twice on
  `sotn-wall-grave-male-name.yaml` ("X Ua Y", Y the father) or `sotn-wall-grave-female-name.yaml` ("X Ui Y", Y the
  mother); `sotn-wall-grave-death-decade.yaml` and `sotn-wall-grave-death-year.yaml` within the room's century;
  `sotn-wall-grave-goods.yaml` once per gp of goods (a repeat becomes result 20, a pile of gold coins over the heart).
- **Totals (p. 15):** 5,548 horizontal graves (3100s 15; 3000s 69; 2900s 144; 2800s 84; 2700s 497 — martyrs of the
  Empyrean repression; 2600s 328; 2500s 1,472 — Chthonic dominance and the Bitter War; 2400s 618; 2300s 356; 2200s
  602; 2100s 636; 2000s 418; 1900s and later 309). All their goods, the skeletal warriors' arms and the troglodytes'
  loot together: **28,000-30,000 gp**.
- **Undead skeleton warriors** ([[Skeleton]]): some graves hold warriors left as guards, in torpor until a stated
  trigger. They take **one round** to animate and climb out (all at once, or in waves, e.g. 1d10 a round), attack every
  living creature in sight, and pursue until the quarry is out of sight or out of the Nethercity. They stay active (waves
  still joining) until the trigger ends and no living creature is in sight of any of the group; then they wait **one
  turn** and return to torpor. In torpor they **cannot be turned** (rebuked) but can be attacked — which wakes the whole
  group. Turned skeletons return to their graves when the effect ends, fighting again if anyone is there. Each carries a
  **bronze short sword and bronze shield** (17 gp new; as loot worth the era's grave-goods value: 1d3 / 1d8 / 4d6 gp;
  not counted in the room tallies).

## Necromancy and the mummies (SOTN pp. 16-17)
The Chthonic faith holds that the soul is consumed at death unless body and soul are kept whole until the **Awakening**,
when the Chthonic gods return and the faithful rise in the flesh. Commoners were embalmed in wall graves; the elite
(nobles, heroes, priests, kings, usually of 9th-12th level) were made **mummies** in sealed tombs. The art improved over
the centuries, which sets each mummy's power by its era:
| Era of mummification | Retains | Example (a 12th-level elven enchanter) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3100-2800 BE | nothing: a plain mummy | 5+1 HD mummy (the warlord Eolande, D) |
| from 2800 BE | ⅓ of HD and class abilities; cultists of **Dirgion** are infested with sarcophagal worms ([[Sepulchral Worm]]) in undeath | 5+1 HD, abilities of a 4th-level enchanter (the mummy transmogrifier, E) |
| from ~2500 BE | ½ | 6+1 HD, 6th-level abilities (the 6 HD mummies of G and H) |
| from ~2200 BE | ⅔ | 8+1 HD, 8th-level abilities (the abyssal mummies, R-S) |
| from 1900 BE | all, with the After the Flesh racial power: keep advancing in undeath (to 14th) | 12+1 HD, 12th level (Q and O) |
| ~1650 BE | mummy lords ([[Mummy Lord]]) | one only: Orthanach, O3 |
A mummy with fewer HD than its era allows died below its maximum level (Cassair and Sorya, O3: 9th level in 1900 BE,
so 9 HD). Class abilities follow SOTN's corrupt-elf classes: [[Elven Cultist]], [[Elven Hierophant]],
[[Elven Warlord]], [[Elven Wizard]] (SOTN pp. 161-173).
- **Armed and armoured mummies (p. 17):** a warrior mummy may use its arms and armour: **AC + the armour and shield**;
  weapon damage **+3** for its strength (plus a fighter's damage bonus where it has one). Weapon blows **do not** carry
  mummy rot (unlike a mummy lord's).
- **Woken mummies:** wrath first, always. One parleyed with, fled from or left alive: `Tables/sotn-mummy-reaction.yaml`
  (1d20: 1-10 exhaustion and back to torpor; then madness, restoration, vengefulness, loneliness, curiosity, hedonism,
  piety, regret, ambition, nihilism). Example (p. 17): the J2 martyr woken and spared might secure the J15 relics
  (restoration), pray in the J8 chancel (piety) or seek the H22 embalmer, buried the same year (loneliness).
- **Mummy statistics** follow the ACKS II [[Mummy]] (AC 6, 5+1**, slam 1d12 + mummy rot, horrific visage, immunities by
  damage type, rebuked as mummies) with SOTN's HD and class abilities added; each room note gives the converted line.

## Treasure (SOTN pp. 17-18)
- Room keys give market value and weight for coins, gems, jewellery and any object worth at least its weight in copper
  (10 gp per st); cheaper objects carried out sell for half their weight in copper. Magic items: apparent value given so
  they can be described without giving them away. Room **tallies count the weight of magic items but not their value**
  (it gives XP only when sold).
- **Attracting attention:** all coin is ancient Argollëan mint and most goods are plainly of that lost civilisation.
  Rare coins and art draw rivals, merchants and thieves: **1% per 2,000 gp spent, sold or deposited**. The Undercity's
  criminal syndicate (SOTN's "Argollëan Brotherhood" = AX3's **Argollëan Family**, [[Organizations of Cyfaraun]]) wants
  the source of any elven antiquities and may hire a rival party to delve for it. Melting coin into ingots or laundering
  through third parties avoids this. In Cyfaraun also: half of Undercity finds are owed to the treasury and tax collectors
  question sellers of antiquities ([[Cyfaraun]], [[Doing Business in Cyfaraun]]).
- **Preservation of antiquity:** the builders' spell ([[SOTN Spells#Preservation of Antiquity]]) means the goods have
  suffered only 150-500 years of decay, not three thousand: archaic in style, remarkably well kept (Art or Craft).
- **Embalming equipment** (bronze hooks, canopic jars, vials of blue natron, funerary urns, charms) counts toward the
  mortuary a necromancer needs (value at least the cost of the necromancy; ACKS II necromancy rules).
- **White ape pelts** (area M): a carcass gives an intact pelt only if the ape was killed by **one attack**, or **only by
  blunt weapons**, or by **cold, death magic, electricity or poison** having lost under half its hp to other attacks.
  Damaged pelts: 1 gp per st. Skinning takes **2 turns** per pelt, **1 turn** with Animal Husbandry, Labor (butchery),
  Labor (farming) or Trapping. (Pelt values: see [[Nethercity M - Northern Caves]].)
